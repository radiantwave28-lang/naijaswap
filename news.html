News Alert 001

Nigerian Charcoal Exports Face Regulatory and Shipping Restrictions

🔴 High Priority Charcoal Aug 2026

Status: Verified — restrictions and shipping complications confirmed; current nationwide ban requires further official confirmation.

Nigeria's charcoal-export sector is facing significant regulatory and shipping challenges. Historical Nigeria Customs directives led major shipping companies to suspend or refuse new charcoal bookings. More recently, charcoal has also been subject to stricter dangerous-goods shipping requirements because of its potential to self-heat and cause fire during maritime transportation.

A particularly relevant case involved 35 charcoal export containers at Lekki Free Trade Terminal, where exporters disputed substantial storage charges. The Nigerian Shippers' Council subsequently reviewed the matter with the terminal and CMA CGM.

What this means for NaijaSwap exporters — before accepting a charcoal export order, sellers should verify:

  • Current Nigeria Customs export authorization
  • Forestry / environmental permits where applicable
  • Shipping-line acceptance of charcoal
  • Dangerous-goods classification and documentation
  • UN1361 / Class 4.2 requirements where applicable
  • Container and cargo compliance
  • Terminal acceptance
  • Export documentation completeness
  • Current shipping-line booking confirmation
⚠ Business Warning: Do not advise a seller to load a container simply because a foreign buyer has issued a purchase order. The exporter should first confirm that the specific shipping line and terminal will accept the charcoal shipment under the applicable DG requirements.
Category: Charcoal / Export Regulation / Shipping Source: Nigeria Customs, CMA CGM, Nigerian Shippers' Council
News Alert 002

Charcoal Containers Can Accumulate Significant Port Storage Costs

🔴 High Priority Port / Logistics Aug 2026

The 2025 Lekki case involving 35 charcoal containers demonstrates the financial risk of containers remaining at terminals while regulatory, documentation or shipping issues are resolved.

The Nigerian Shippers' Council reported that the affected containers were valued at more than ₦70 million, and the dispute involved storage charges of ₦30,240 per container per day.

📝 NaijaSwap Recommendation: For every charcoal shipment, your CRM should eventually track: Buyer → Seller → Product → Quantity → Container number → Terminal → Shipping line → DG status → Customs status → Permit status → Booking status → Gate-in date → Free-storage expiry → Current status. That makes your NaijaSwap database much more useful than simply keeping buyer names and telephone numbers.
Category: Port / Logistics / Charcoal Source: Nigerian Shippers' Council, Lekki Free Trade Terminal
News Alert 003

Nigerian Agricultural Exports Continue Strong Growth

🟢 Positive Agriculture Aug 2026

Nigeria Customs reported that ₦767.2 billion ($568.27 million) worth of agricultural products were exported through the Lilypond Export Command between October and December 2025.

Products included: cashew, sesame, hibiscus, cocoa, ginger, soybeans, charcoal, processed wood, rubber and groundnuts.

✅ Pre-Shipment Compliance Checklist

🇺🇳 Nigeria Customs

  • Valid export authorization for the specific commodity
  • Harmonized System (HS) code classification
  • Single Goods Declaration (SGD) filed
  • Customs release and exit note obtained
Tip: Charcoal may require additional scrutiny — confirm current directive status before booking.

🌳 Forestry & Environment

  • Forestry permit (where applicable for wood/charcoal)
  • Environmental Impact Assessment (if required)
  • Sustainable sourcing documentation
  • State ministry of environment clearance
Tip: Some states have additional restrictions on charcoal production.

🚢 Shipping & DG

  • Shipping line acceptance letter for charcoal
  • Dangerous Goods declaration (if Class 4.2)
  • UN1361 classification and packaging compliance
  • Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS)
  • Temperature monitoring plan for self-heating risk
Tip: Not all lines accept charcoal. Confirm before container gate-in.

📄 Export Documentation

  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Certificate of Origin (NEPC or Chamber of Commerce)
  • Phytosanitary certificate (for agricultural products)
  • Quality / inspection certificate (SGS, Bureau Veritas)
  • Bill of Lading (or Sea Waybill)
Tip: European buyers often require SGS or equivalent pre-shipment inspection.

📦 Container & Terminal

  • Container suitability (ventilated for charcoal if required)
  • Terminal acceptance confirmation
  • Gate-in appointment scheduled
  • Free storage period confirmed in writing
  • Demurrage / detention rate schedule reviewed
Tip: Lekki case shows storage can cost ₦30,240/day per container. Track free time.

🌎 Buyer-Side Requirements

  • Import license (buyer country)
  • EU TRACES registration (for agri-food into Europe)
  • Charcoal import permit (if buyer country restricts)
  • Letter of Credit or escrow terms agreed
  • Incoterms 2020 agreed (FOB, CIF, DAP, etc.)
Tip: NaijaSwap escrow protects both parties when terms are documented.
