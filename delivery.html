International Shipping for B2B Export

Step-by-step ocean freight, air cargo, and documentation guidance for exporting from Nigeria to Europe, Asia, and the Americas.

📋 Standard Nigerian Export Payment Terms

  • 70–80% paid against B/L Copy — The bulk of payment is released only when the seller provides a copy of the Bill of Lading (proof of shipment). No large advance payment required.
  • 20–30% final payment on arrival & inspection — The balance is held in escrow and released only after goods arrive at destination and the buyer confirms quality and quantity through inspection.
  • These terms are stated in the Sales Contract from day one and apply to all B2B export transactions on NaijaSwap.
Why this matters: This is the industry-standard payment structure for Nigerian commodity exports. It protects both buyer and seller — the seller ships with confidence knowing payment is secured against B/L, while the buyer retains a holdback to ensure goods match specifications on arrival. NaijaSwap escrow enforces these terms automatically.

📋 Export Guidelines — Before You Ship

  • Ensure your company is registered with CAC and has a valid NEPC export certificate
  • Verify product quality with SGS, Bureau Veritas, or Intertek before shipment
  • Confirm buyer's payment structure is locked in the Sales Contract: 70–80% against B/L copy, 20–30% on arrival & inspection
  • Obtain all required export permits from Nigerian Customs (NCS) and SON where applicable
  • Pack goods according to international shipping standards — ISPM-15 for wooden pallets
  • Label all packages with HS codes, country of origin, and handling instructions
  • Insure cargo with marine insurance (110% of CIF value recommended)
  • Never ship without confirmed payment terms or signed contract

World's largest container carrier. Weekly sailings from Lagos (Apapa/Tincan) to Rotterdam, Hamburg, and Felixstowe. FCL and LCL available.

Second-largest global carrier. Direct service to Mediterranean and Northern Europe. Competitive 20ft/40ft container rates.

🚢 CMA CGM

French line with strong West Africa presence. Reefer containers for temperature-sensitive agri-exports. Regular service to France and Spain.

🚢 Grimaldi Lines

Specialist in RoRo and container freight from West Africa to Europe. Preferred partner for charcoal, vehicles, and bulk commodities.

Air freight for high-value, time-sensitive shipments. Door-to-door with customs brokerage. Best for samples and urgent orders.

🚢 PIL (Pacific International)

Asian-focused carrier with Nigeria connections. Good rates for exports to China, India, and Southeast Asia.

Lagos → Asia: 25-35 days
Step-by-Step International Shipping Process

Step 1: Order Confirmation & Contract Signing

Buyer issues Purchase Order (PO). Seller confirms availability and pricing. Both parties sign the Sales Contract with Incoterm, payment terms (70–80% against B/L copy; 20–30% on arrival & inspection), and delivery schedule. NaijaSwap escrow is initiated.

Day 0-2

Step 2: Production & Quality Preparation

Supplier processes, sorts, and packages goods according to contract specifications. Independent inspection (SGS/Bureau Veritas) arranged if required by buyer or contract terms.

Day 3-14

Step 3: Booking & Documentation

Seller books vessel space with chosen shipping line (Maersk, MSC, CMA CGM, Grimaldi). Prepares export documents: Commercial Invoice, Packing List, Certificate of Origin, SONCAP (if required), and Export Permit from NCS.

Day 10-16

Step 4: Customs Clearance & Loading

Goods transported to port (Apapa, Tincan, Onne, or Calabar). Nigerian Customs clears export declaration. Container is loaded onto vessel. Shipping line issues Bill of Lading (B/L).

Day 14-18

Step 5: B/L Copy & First Payment Milestone (70–80%)

Seller uploads B/L copy to NaijaSwap. Buyer verifies shipment details. 70–80% of contract value is released from escrow to seller upon B/L verification. This is the standard Nigerian export payment practice — no large advance payment was required.

Day 18-20

Step 6: Ocean Transit & Tracking

Vessel departs Nigerian port. Buyer receives original B/L and can track shipment via shipping line website. Goods are in transit to destination port.

Day 20-40

Step 7: Arrival & Destination Customs

Goods arrive at destination port (Rotterdam, Hamburg, Felixstowe, etc.). Buyer's customs broker clears import declaration. Duties and VAT paid by buyer.

Day 40-45

Step 8: Inspection & Final Payment (20–30%)

Buyer inspects quality and quantity against contract specifications. If satisfactory, remaining 20–30% balance is released from escrow to seller. If discrepancies are found, the holdback covers remediation or dispute resolution.

Day 45-48

Step 9: Final Delivery

Goods transported from port to buyer's warehouse or factory. Delivery receipt signed. Transaction complete. Both parties leave feedback on NaijaSwap.

Day 48-55

Nigerian Export Ports

⚓ Lagos (Apapa / Tincan)

Nigeria's busiest port handling 70% of maritime trade. Best for containerized cargo. Direct connections to Europe, Asia, and Americas.

  • • Container terminals: APMT, Tincan Island
  • • Best for: General cargo, containers, vehicles
  • • Shipping lines: Maersk, MSC, CMA CGM, Grimaldi

⚓ Port Harcourt (Onne)

Deep-water port serving the oil and gas industry. Also handles agricultural exports from the Niger Delta region.

  • • Free Zone status available
  • • Best for: Oil & gas, agri-exports from South-South
  • • Shipping lines: Maersk, MSC, PIL

⚓ Calabar Port

Strategic port for exports to Central and West African markets. Less congested than Lagos. Good for bulk agricultural commodities.

  • • Dry bulk terminal available
  • • Best for: Cocoa, palm oil, rubber, timber
  • • Shipping lines: Grimaldi, regional carriers

⚓ Warri Port

Serves the Midwest and Niger Delta regions. Handles general cargo and bulk commodities. Good alternative to Lagos congestion.

  • • River port with draft limitations
  • • Best for: Local bulk cargo, regional trade
  • • Shipping lines: Regional and coastal vessels
